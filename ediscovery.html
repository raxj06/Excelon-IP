eDiscovery Overview

The process of E-discovery comprises many actions altogether which begin with litigation and end when the documents are presented in a court of law by both parties. Once litigation is instituted, a legal hold is implemented on both parties.

Under a legal hold, both sides are legally responsible to preserve and retain relevant information and documents in an electronic format. Legal attorneys from both fronts make requests for E-discovery. Once a framework is set, the data from different electronic sources is collected to be presented in court as potential evidence. However, before preserving every document, it is important to determine the relevancy of the documents.

Legal Hold Compliance

Attorneys use various methods for identifying the relevancy of records to ensure that no critical data is lost and that only legally relevant files are processed.

eDiscovery in ANDA Litigation

A similar process also happens when there is the litigation of ANDA (Abbreviated New Drug Application) products in the USA.

However, in e-discovery of ANDA litigation, certain strategies must be implemented to reduce the unnecessary cost of collecting, storing, and processing huge databases. At Excelon IP, we provide highly efficient and cost-effective strategies designed for the process of E-discovery.

The Excelon IP team reviews all documents, removes duplicate copies and non-essential data, minimizing the costs that would otherwise be extremely expensive to the clients if they pursued the process of E-discovery without a well-formed strategy.

Evidence Production & Case Factors

Once data is collected, relevant documents are identified, and storage costs are optimized, it must be decided how the documents will be produced in court as potential evidence at different stages during a trial.

Various factors influence the activity of E-discovery, including:

1
Nature & Size: The overall dimensions and commercial reach of the business entity.
2
Industry Classification: The specific industry sector and regulatory rules of the organization.
3
Data Structure: The internal structure of electronic data storage within the company.
4
Nature of Dispute: The specific legal claims and arguments raised during litigation.
